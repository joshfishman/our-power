# Public-Support Audit — 28 Federal Scorecard Positions

**Purpose.** This file documents the best available public-opinion evidence for each of the 28 federal policy positions in the Common Ground scorecard. The scorecard only includes a position if a **majority of the public supports it**, so each row records support %, partisan split, pollster + date, exact question wording, a **match-confidence** flag (does the poll measure THIS position or a broader/narrower proxy?), and a source URL.

**Reading the match-confidence column.**
- **HIGH** — a credible poll asked about essentially this exact policy.
- **MEDIUM** — a credible poll asked about a closely related but broader/narrower concept; directionally reliable but not a bill-level match.
- **LOW** — no direct poll exists; the number is a proxy (e.g., "donors have too much influence") and should be treated cautiously.

**Caveat on sourcing.** Many of the strongest numbers come from advocacy-commissioned polls (Data for Progress, SEIA, BPC Action, First Five Years Fund, social-welfare orgs). These are cross-checked against neutral pollsters (Pew, KFF, AP-NORC, Quinnipiac, Gallup, Program for Public Consultation/UMD, Ipsos, YouGov) wherever possible. Question framing materially moves several of these numbers — flagged inline.

Compiled June 2026. No numbers are fabricated; where no credible poll exists this is stated explicitly.

## Gate Analysis

Threshold = share of U.S. adults / RV / LV supporting the position in the best-matched poll. Positions with only proxy/legislative evidence are flagged.

### At the 50% threshold — PASS / FAIL

**PASS (clear majority):** 3, 4, 5 (Plank 1); 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12 (Plank 2); 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18 (Plank 3); 19, 20, 21, 22, 23 (Plank 4); 25, 26, 28 (Plank 5).

**AMBIGUOUS / framing-dependent at 50%:** 2 (public financing ~60% only when described as voluntary; bare "taxpayer-funded campaigns" framings poll lower), 27 (antitrust — passes on "rein in Big Tech" framing, FAILS on bill-specific Morning Consult provisions).

**NO POLL (cannot gate):** 1 (corporate PAC — only an 80% "too much influence" proxy), 24 (PACT Act — legislative vote only).

### At the 55% threshold

Everything that passed 50% with a real majority still clears 55% **except**: position 28 (trade-labor, ~55% — right at the line), and position 2 (public financing, ~60% but soft). Position 27 (antitrust) only clears on the favorable framing.

### At the 60% threshold

**Comfortably clears 60%:** 3 (85), 4 (65), 5 (86), 6 (~65), 7 (83), 8 (~86), 9 (~84), 10 (~70+), 11 (83), 12 (74), 13 (82), 14 (~65), 16 (77), 17 (83), 18 (62–67), 19 (76), 20 (76), 22 (85), 23 (82+), 25 (83), 26 (70).

**Borderline / fails 60%:** 15 (non-compete 59–66, depends on poll), 28 (trade-labor ~55 — **FAILS 60**), 2 (public financing ~60 — at the line), 27 (antitrust — **FAILS 60** on bill-specific evidence).

**Cannot evaluate at 60%:** 1 (no direct poll), 24 (no poll).

### Plank-5 read: popular-but-partisan vs. genuinely weak

This is where the cross-partisan promise is most tested.

- **#25 Pentagon audit — popular AND genuinely bipartisan.** 83% with R 82 / D 84 / I 84. This is one of the most cross-partisan positions in the entire scorecard. Strong keep.
- **#26 War powers — popular overall (70%) but the most partisan item here.** Headline support is high, but the split is severe: ~91% of Democrats vs. only ~36% of Republicans say Congress must approve (i.e., 62% of Republicans say the president does NOT need approval). It clears the 50/60 gate on aggregate, but it is **popular-but-partisan**, not consensus. Framing and the party of the sitting president swing Republican answers heavily.
- **#27 Antitrust / Open App Markets — genuinely weak at the bill level.** "Rein in Big Tech" polls 74–85%, but the *actual* Open App Markets Act provisions never exceeded ~32% in Morning Consult, "keep businesses from getting too big" is a 16% priority, and Pew shows overall appetite for tech regulation *declining* (44%, down from 56%). This is the **shakiest position in the scorecard** — the popular-sounding framing masks soft, framing-dependent, and possibly sub-majority bill-level support. Candidate to cut or re-scope (e.g., to "stop app-store self-preferencing," which polls better than the bill as a whole).
- **#28 Trade-labor standards — moderate and soft (~55%).** Passes 50, fails 60, trade polling is volatile and tariff-contaminated. Popular-ish but not robust; flag.

### Weakest public support — candidates to cut or flag (3–5)

1. **#27 Antitrust / Open App Markets Act** — strongest "popular framing vs. weak bill reality" gap; bill-level support may be sub-majority. **Strongest cut/re-scope candidate.**
2. **#1 Corporate-PAC refusal** — *no direct poll exists*; only an 80% "donors have too much influence" proxy. Cannot be gated honestly. Flag and re-scope to the disclosure/influence framing that actually polls.
3. **#24 PACT Act** — *no public-opinion poll located*; only the 86–11 Senate vote as a proxy. Likely popular but uncited; flag as evidence-gap.
4. **#28 Trade-labor standards** — ~55%, fails the 60 gate, volatile. Flag.
5. **#2 Public financing** — ~60% only with favorable "voluntary small-donor matching" framing; bare "taxpayer-funded elections" framings drop below majority. Borderline; flag the framing sensitivity.

### Positions with NO solid polling (be explicit)

- **#1 Corporate-PAC refusal** — no poll on the actual position; proxy only.
- **#24 PACT Act / burn-pit care** — no public-opinion poll found; legislative-vote proxy only.
- **#14 / #19 (GOP-led alternatives)** — no poll names the Hawley wage bill or treats the GOP paid-leave tax-credit approach as distinct; both ride on the broader $15-wage (65%) and paid-leave (76–82%) numbers. Reasonable but not bill-specific.
